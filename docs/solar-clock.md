# Interactive solar clock

The daily view uses the same request and calculation result as the existing timetable and exports. The WASM bridge adds two read-only queries. `solarPosition` evaluates the existing Rust solar model at one Unix timestamp and returns geometric altitude and true-north azimuth in degrees. `civilDayBounds` resolves consecutive local midnights using the engine's embedded IANA data. The SVG trajectory samples the selected day and includes the solver's unrounded rise, transit and set instants. The prayer buttons use the timetable's configured adjustments and rounding. Only markers at visible solar altitudes appear on the sky trajectory.

The slider runs in chronological order from the start of the selected civil day to the next midnight. It skips nonexistent local times in spring and includes both occurrences of a repeated local time in autumn. The clock shows the UTC offset when the civil-day duration changes. Historical and future dates begin in simulation at solar transit. **Now** clears the simulation and selects the current date in the configured timezone. Live rendering reads `Date.now()` in an animation frame callback, with fewer updates when reduced motion is enabled. The countdown uses the adjusted absolute prayer instant and consults adjacent dates for prayers across midnight.

The scene uses a single oblique projection with east on the left and north toward the foreground. The sun's horizontal components follow the azimuth and its vertical component follows altitude. For a figure of height \(H\) and positive solar altitude \(\alpha\), the ground shadow has length \(H / \tan(\alpha)\) and azimuth equal to the sun's plus 180°. The shadow polygon applies fractions of that full displacement to the head, shoulders, arms, and legs, with the base fixed at the feet. The SVG smoothly compresses long shadows to fit inside the floor radius. Educational measurements use the uncapped length. The visible solar disc follows the engine's conventional −0.833° sunrise and sunset threshold. The geometric shadow disappears at or below 0°.

The Asr panel displays the measured shadow at transit, the selected school factor, and the threshold evaluated from the Rust solver's target altitude. The solver accounts for the declination change during the afternoon, so the threshold can differ slightly from the geometric shadow at transit plus the school multiple of the figure's height. When no Asr crossing exists, the threshold is unavailable.

Run `node scripts/test-solar.mjs` after the WASM build. It exercises the WASM queries, trajectory and projection, both Asr factors, the slider and live time selection, southern and northern hemispheres, polar day and night, and both DST transitions. The existing native-to-WASM parity suite continues to cover prayer calculations.
